Doug is doing security onboarding with Blake. Having taking the embarrassing staff photo, Doug runs through the rules for door badges.

"There's no FINCO logo on your door badge," Doug explains. "So, if you lose it, people won't know they can use it here..."

Doug hands over the badge, which is attached to a distinctive lanyard with the FINCO logo repeat-printed on it.

"Oh," Doug adds. "And we don't allow personalized lanyards."

:::

:::div{.note}

I just wanted to add to this cartoon a note, because the premise of this joke *seems* too ridiculous... right?! Well, I joined a truly pathological organization where folks only shouted at each other, and also complained if you raised your voice. I was given exactly this kind of badge. Plain white. No information to identify where the badge could be used. If you lose it and someone finds it, they wouldn't know where the badge could be used.

However, it did, in fact, come with a company branded lanyard. I switched the lanyard out for a Microsoft "ALLY" lanyard, the rainbow-themed lanyard was part of the diversity and inclusion initiative Microsoft was running at the time. I got in trouble twice a week about this. "YOU SHOULD USE THE COMPANY-BRANDED LANYARD," the compliance manager would shout at me (I told you about the shouting), even though they were also the person telling everyone that the logo wasn't on the badge in case it got lost.

I was reminded on this bizarre real world story during a conversation on social media.

:::
